# Rent one 96 GB GPU, test Nemotron first

Rent a single **x86 RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell 96 GB** (RunPod: $1.69/h community, $2.49/h secure, per-second billing, free egress) and run a focused bake-off. Its centre is **NVIDIA Nemotron‑3.5‑Transcribe‑30B‑A4B**. That model takes up to an hour of audio per call. It is the only open speech-LLM trained to ignore listed terms that were not spoken, and it holds about 98% keyword precision with whole-session term lists ([Nemotron 3.5 report](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/nemotron/files/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3.5-Transcribe-Technical-Report.pdf)). The second candidate is **freetranscribe/parakeet‑ft‑v1**, a CC‑BY Parakeet‑v3 fine-tune, which since 26 October is the best open model on the leaderboard's long-form track (9.81 WER; CORAAL 12.35) ([HF Open ASR Leaderboard](https://huggingface.co/spaces/hf-audio/open_asr_leaderboard)). The third is **Qwen3‑ASR‑1.7B served by vLLM**, which still has the best held-out (private) WER of any open model and becomes cheap at cloud speed. Granite Speech 4.1‑2B‑Plus, VibeVoice‑ASR, MOSS‑Transcribe‑Diarize, Cohere Transcribe and Voxtral Small are worth one or two runs each as challengers or voting partners. Most other "big" speech-LLMs score worse than Whisper on spontaneous speech and can be skipped. The roughly 30-run gold-set bake-off below should cost about **$20–$40** of GPU time, mostly spent on environment setup. A full 6,500 h pass with the top three systems costs about **$180–$350**, and Nemotron alone accounts for $100–$230 of that. The decisive caveat is that no public benchmark resembles cassette-era dharma talks. The project's own Phase 2 bake-off still has no corrected references, so every ranking here stays provisional until the user's corrected references give WER, term precision and term recall ([ASR bake-off](ASR%20bake-off.md)).

## Only four candidates truly need rented memory

Most of the models worth testing are small. Parakeet derivatives (0.6B), MOSS (0.9B), Qwen3‑ASR (1.7B), Granite (2B) and Cohere (2B) all fit the home RTX 3070 Ti's 8 GB. The cloud is needed for two other reasons. The first is memory, for Nemotron‑3.5 (~75 GB in bf16 under vLLM, per a third-party measurement ([aicybr](https://aicybr.com/blog/nvidia-nemotron-3-5-transcribe-local-speech-diarization-guide))), VibeVoice‑ASR 9B, Voxtral Small 24B and Qwen3‑Omni‑30B. The second is throughput, for Qwen3‑ASR, which crawls at 17–22× real time under transformers at home but reaches about 380× under stock vLLM on an RTX PRO 6000 ([Baseten](https://baseten.co/blog/fastest-lowest-cost-qwen3-asr-batch-transcription)). The ranking below weighs evidence relevant to 30–90-minute spontaneous monologues: held-out ("private") WER, the noisy/reverberant URGENT24 set, long-form WER (Earnings and CORAAL field interviews), single-pass length, context mechanism and hallucination evidence. Leaderboard figures are the 10 November 2026 snapshot (v1.15.1) ([HF Open ASR Leaderboard](https://huggingface.co/spaces/hf-audio/open_asr_leaderboard)).

**Nemotron leads because its context mechanism was built for exactly this problem.** 15.5% of its training examples were contextual ASR. These included "distractor" lists (spoken terms plus similar-sounding unspoken ones) and "negative" lists (only absent terms), which per NVIDIA "teach the model to ignore listed terms that are not spoken." On Contextual Earnings‑22, keyword recall rose from 58% to 96% with clip-local lists and to 90% with the whole call's inventory, **while precision stayed at 98%**. Keyword F1 went 73.0 → 97.1 (local) → 93.5 (global). Qwen3‑ASR‑1.7B scored 69 / 92 / 87 on the same test ([Nemotron 3.5 report](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/nemotron/files/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3.5-Transcribe-Technical-Report.pdf)). That precision figure goes straight at the project's two observed failures, Whisper large‑v3 echoing its prompt in 12 of 38 excerpts and Parakeet boosting turning "koan" into "Khan" ([bake-off findings](../eval/findings.md)). Nemotron is also second on long-form (10.52) and handles a full hour per call. It still has risks. Its card warns that "error rates can exceed 100% with many insertions" and that generation can loop in rare cases ([Nemotron card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3.5-Transcribe-30B-A4B)). The chat template's reasoning mode must also be disabled, or "the model generates a reasoning trace that ignores the audio" ([Nemotron 3.5 report](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/nemotron/files/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3.5-Transcribe-Technical-Report.pdf)). Every one of its contextual-ASR numbers comes from NVIDIA, and nobody has yet tested it independently with distractor lists.

**parakeet‑ft‑v1 is the cheapest high-value swap, and it does not need the cloud.** It keeps v3's architecture and tokenizer and loads with `ASRModel.from_pretrained`, so the existing `asr/run_parakeet.py --model freetranscribe/parakeet-ft-v1` should work with GPU‑PB boosting unchanged. That is an inference, not something tested. One developer trained it on 100 h of self-labelled interviews, podcasts and talks. On 16 held-out long recordings, v3 skipped 87 passages and this model skipped 3. Its card is candid that "names and jargon are no more accurate than with v3", so boosting remains essential ([parakeet‑ft‑v1 card](https://huggingface.co/freetranscribe/parakeet-ft-v1)). Its decoding recipe also improves stock v3, with no retraining. Earnings‑22 WER fell from 13.75 to 11.12 after cutting at pauses into ≤90 s pieces with 5 s overlap and widening local attention to 256 frames. That is a reminder that chunking can move WER as much as swapping models.

**Qwen3‑ASR stays a finalist on robustness, not on biasing.** It has the lowest private WER of any open model. A newer community GRPO fine-tune (Musci) tops short-form at 5.70 but is no better on private data (7.64 vs 7.58) and is worse on URGENT24 ([Musci card](https://huggingface.co/Musci-research/Musci-ASR-qwen-1019-hf)), so it is only a side check. An independent perturbation study found Qwen3‑ASR produced 1,353 insertions (3% repetition) under 30% signal masking, against Whisper large‑v3's 10,365 (35% repetition). Granite, however, had 49–57% repetition, which the authors blame on its compressed Q‑former encoder ([Do LLM Decoders Listen Fairly?](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.21276v1)). The weakness is distractors. With 250 distractor terms, Qwen3‑ASR's biased-word error on YouTube speech doubled from 24.52 to 48.32 ([Huber & Waibel](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.05759)). That fits the local finding that it echoes prose prompts but not short term lists.

**The challengers each bring a testable feature rather than a better average.** Granite 4.1‑2B‑Plus is the only new model with published keyword-biasing gains. Its keyword F1 rose by 5–20 points (Gigaspeech 74.1 → 84.4, TED‑LIUM 88.7 → 94.3). It also accepts "incremental decoding" conditioned on previous segments and outputs speaker-attributed text ([Granite 4.1‑2B‑Plus card](https://huggingface.co/ibm-granite/granite-speech-4.1-2b-plus)). VibeVoice‑ASR achieved the best biased WER of any model with correct-only lists (Yodas 4.17), but it collapsed to 27.92 with 250 distractors. Without context it was worse than Whisper on spontaneous speech (Earnings‑21 16.71 vs 13.52) ([Huber & Waibel](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.05759)). MOSS takes 90 minutes per call with diarization and hotwords, but it has no long-form English numbers ([MOSS card](https://huggingface.co/OpenMOSS-Team/MOSS-Transcribe-Diarize)). Cohere has the best open URGENT24 score (9.50) and no biasing, which makes it a natural no-context voting partner. Voxtral Small's open weights document no context input, and its 55 GB footprint buys only a second opinion.

**Several models fail on spontaneous English or on licence terms.** Qwen3‑Omni‑30B scored 22.57 on Earnings‑21 against Whisper large‑v2's 13.52, but as a per-word presence judge it reached 94–99% recall ([Huber & Waibel](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.05759)), so it belongs in the pipeline as a filter, not a transcriber. Canary‑Qwen‑2.5B was trained on ≤40 s audio ([Canary‑Qwen card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/canary-qwen-2.5b)). On a deliberately hard Earnings‑22 subset, Canary‑1B and Parakeet‑TDT‑v2 hallucinated in about a third of segments, more often than Whisper large‑v3 at 23.8% ([HALAS](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.23048)). The 2026 newcomers ARK‑ASR, Hojo‑ASR and Higgs‑Audio‑v3 combine high benchmark-fitting rates with weaker URGENT24 and private scores. CrisperWhisper 2.0, the Zipformer‑XL and Audio Flamingo 3 carry non-commercial licences ([CrisperWhisper 2.0 card](https://huggingface.co/nyralabs/CrisperWhisper2.0_large); [Zipformer card](https://huggingface.co/soundsgoodai/Zipformer-cr-ctc-transducer-XL-290M); [AF3 licence](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/audio-flamingo/blob/main/LICENSE)). Gemma 4 caps audio at 30 s ([Gemma audio docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/docs/capabilities/audio)). The two autumn releases with built-in hotwords and diarization, Qwen‑Audio‑3.1‑ASR and Voxtral 3, appear to be API-only despite aggregator listings. The Voxtral 3 claim rests on a single source ([arXiv 2610.28711](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.28711v1); [MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/10/20/mistral-voxtral-3-speech-models-voice-agent-sdk/)).

## Five environments run every candidate on one x86 card

The hardware question resolves to one number: about **96 GB of VRAM covers everything at bf16**. Nemotron's official recipe targets "one H100 80 GB" with `--gpu-memory-utilization 0.94` ([NeMo docs](https://docs.nvidia.com/nemo/speech/nightly/speechlm2/nemotron_transcribe.html)), and a third party measured "just over 75GB" during inference ([aicybr](https://aicybr.com/blog/nvidia-nemotron-3-5-transcribe-local-speech-diarization-guide)). That is tight on 80 GB and comfortable on 96 GB. Smaller formats exist. The FP8 checkpoint runs on an L40S 48 GB, NVFP4 is Blackwell-only ([Nemotron card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3.5-Transcribe-30B-A4B)), and the IQ4_NL GGUF the user already holds is 19.9 GiB including the encoder ([openclawdc](https://openclawdc.com/blog/can-i-run-nemotron-3-5-transcribe-locally/)). Voxtral Small needs about 55 GB at bf16 ([Voxtral Small card](https://huggingface.co/mistralai/Voxtral-Small-24B-2507)). Qwen3‑Omni's vendor minimum is 79 GB, though that was measured for 15 s of video, not audio ([Qwen3‑Omni card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B-Instruct)). VibeVoice runs in 24 GB, with 32–48 GB comfortable for 60-minute single passes. GPU generation matters for quantized paths. vLLM runs FP8 compute only on SM 8.9+ (Ada, Hopper, Blackwell) and falls back to weight-only Marlin kernels on Ampere, which saves memory but not time ([vLLM FP8 docs](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/features/quantization/llm_compressor/fp8/)).

vLLM 0.33.0 is now the common serving layer. Its `/v1/audio/transcriptions` list covers Whisper, Voxtral, Qwen3‑ASR, Qwen3‑Omni (thinker), Granite Speech 4.1‑2B‑Plus, Canary‑1B‑v2, Cohere Transcribe, MOSS‑Transcribe‑Diarize, Nemotron‑3.5‑Transcribe and VibeVoice‑ASR‑HF. Parakeet‑TDT is served through a plugin ([vLLM supported models](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/models/supported_models.html)). Version pins still conflict, so one environment cannot hold everything. Nemotron wants **vLLM 0.32.0 plus NeMo Speech `main`, Python 3.13 and `av==19.0.2`** ([NeMo docs](https://docs.nvidia.com/nemo/speech/nightly/speechlm2/nemotron_transcribe.html)), which clashes with this repo's `av<19` cap for faster-whisper. Microsoft's VibeVoice plugin pins vLLM 0.14.1, because plain `vllm serve microsoft/VibeVoice-ASR` failed ([vLLM #39964](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/39964)). Voxtral needs `mistral-common[audio]`.

That means five environments, following the repo's existing `asr/envs/*` pattern. The main env keeps faster-whisper. `asr/envs/nemo` keeps Parakeet, parakeet‑ft‑v1 and NGPU‑LM. A new `asr/envs/nemotron` holds the Nemotron vLLM 0.32 stack. A new `asr/envs/vllm` on vLLM 0.33 serves Qwen3‑ASR, Granite, Cohere, MOSS, Voxtral, Qwen3‑Omni and VibeVoice‑ASR‑HF. NVIDIA's NeMo‑Speech.cpp, from its NGC container, runs the GGUF Nemotron quant. Its batched CUDA path needs CUDA 12.8+ ([openclawdc](https://openclawdc.com/blog/can-i-run-nemotron-3-5-transcribe-locally/); [NGC](https://catalog.ngc.nvidia.com/orgs/nvidia/-/containers/nemo-speech.cpp/-)).

Two vLLM settings will silently break long audio if left at their defaults. The default audio decode cap is 600 s, so set `VLLM_MAX_AUDIO_DECODE_DURATION_S=3600` and `VLLM_MAX_AUDIO_CLIP_FILESIZE_MB=180` for hour-long talks ([Nemotron card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3.5-Transcribe-30B-A4B)). Separately, Qwen3‑ASR requests over about 10 minutes do not fit under stock vLLM ([Baseten](https://baseten.co/blog/fastest-lowest-cost-qwen3-asr-batch-transcription)), so Qwen3‑ASR needs the VAD chunking the project already uses.

Throughput figures are uneven and mostly vendor-measured. Parakeet runs at ~3,300× on the leaderboard's A100. Nemotron reaches 270–410× on an H100 with tuned vLLM and speculative decoding. NVIDIA says the open-source vLLM path is "roughly two times slower" than its NIM ([Nemotron card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3.5-Transcribe-30B-A4B)). Qwen3‑ASR plateaus near 380× on stock vLLM ([Baseten](https://baseten.co/blog/fastest-lowest-cost-qwen3-asr-batch-transcription)), and Whisper large‑v3 runs at ~146× ([Open ASR paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06961v3)). None of these were measured on hour-long lecture files, so the bake-off should record `wall_sec` on the cloud card and recompute full-run costs from it.

The architecture choice has one trap. The **GH200's ARM64 host cannot run the faster-whisper baseline on GPU**. The CTranslate2 4.9.0 aarch64 wheel is CPU-only and reports "not compiled with CUDA support" ([PyPI ctranslate2](https://pypi.org/project/ctranslate2/4.9.0/#files); [speaches #620](https://github.com/speaches-ai/speaches/issues/620)), while torch, vLLM 0.33 and SGLang now ship aarch64 wheels ([PyPI vllm](https://pypi.org/project/vllm/)). The RTX PRO 6000 has its own unverified risk. It is a Blackwell sm_120 part, and nobody has confirmed whether the repo's cu12 CTranslate2 wheels run on it, so a five-minute `asr/run_whisper.py --limit 2` smoke test should come first. NVFP4 on sm_120 is also unconfirmed, so use bf16 or FP8 there.

## Distractor-trained prompts and tuned boosting beat bigger models

The literature agrees with the project's own results: **how context is delivered matters more than model size**, and models differ chiefly in how they handle terms that are listed but not spoken.

### Prompt formats: short local lists, trained templates, echo checks in code

For speech-LLMs without distractor training, a 250-term list raised biased-word error 39–570% relative, mostly through insertions of list words. These models also favour terms placed early in the prompt ([Huber & Waibel](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.05759)). Even so, a list still roughly halves rare-word errors against no context at all. Without context, YouTube BWER was 56.80 for Qwen3‑ASR, which is worse than its 48.32 with distractors. The practical rules follow from that.

**Give each chunk only its likely terms, most likely first.** Use the model's own trained template. For Nemotron that is "This audio may contain the following words or phrases (some may not be present in the clip): …" ([Nemotron 3.5 report](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/nemotron/files/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3.5-Transcribe-Technical-Report.pdf)). For Granite it is "Keywords: …". Never give prose to LLM decoders. Whisper is the exception: spoken-style prose prompts reduced hallucination relative to bare lists ([CB‑Whisper](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.09552v3)), which matches turbo's clean 13% → 3% result in the project's bake-off.

**Detect echo and loops in code.** Very long lists invite leakage. One study found 460+ names in a Phi‑4‑mini prompt "may leak the whole phrase list" ([LOGIC](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.15397v1)). Reuse Qwen3‑ASR's `detect_and_fix_repetitions` guardrail. Nemotron adopted it and it changed 7 of 451 long-form recordings, but it "cannot recover speech that the model skipped while looping" ([Nemotron 3.5 report](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/nemotron/files/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3.5-Transcribe-Technical-Report.pdf)). Re-decode flagged windows without the prompt.

**A two-stage filter rescues weak models but costs strong ones.** Asking Qwen3‑Omni whether each term is present cut YouTube BWER at 250 distractors from 27.92 to 8.71 for VibeVoice. With a perfect list, the same filter raised it from 4.17 to 8.17 ([Huber & Waibel](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.05759)). Test it only on the models that collapse with distractors.

### Decode-time biasing: the untested levers are beam search and an unknown-token score

NeMo GPU‑PB works on CTC, TDT/RNN‑T and Canary AED models without retraining ([NeMo word boosting docs](https://docs.nvidia.com/nemo-framework/user-guide/latest/nemotoolkit/asr/asr_customization/word_boosting.html)). In NVIDIA's TurboBias paper, RNN‑T **beam** search with a 200-phrase list raised key-phrase F-score from 44.2 to 82.9 (recall 29 → 76, precision 97 → 90) and cut WER from 12.8 to 9.6, with throughput only 2–5% lower. **Greedy decoding gains much less** (42.5 → 70.4). The paper controls over-boosting in three ways. It sets the unknown-token score `c_unk` close to the context score, where the default is 0. It tunes the fusion weight on a dev set. And it drops phrases the baseline already gets right more than 70% of the time. A 20K-phrase list with the weight lowered to 0.7 cost 2.7 F-points and 1.6 WER points against the 200-phrase list, from extra false accepts ([TurboBias](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.07014v2)).

The project's runner boosts with greedy decoding, `context_score` 1.0, `depth_scaling` 2.0 and the default `c_unk`. Beam search plus a raised `c_unk`, along with dropping terms Parakeet already spells correctly, is therefore the obvious untested fix for "koan" → "Khan". All of this runs on any GPU that runs Parakeet, including the home card.

Trie-based logit biasing for speech-LLMs (LOGIC) cut entity WER by 9% relative with only +0.3% false alarms ([LOGIC](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.15397v1)). No public code was found, so it stays a research option.

### N-gram LM fusion: small gains and a deletion risk

N-gram fusion helps only modestly. In NVIDIA's air-traffic-control guide, an n-gram trained on about 5K domain words moved fine-tuned Parakeet from 19.72% to 19.14% WER ([NVIDIA HF blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/nvidia/fine-tune-asr-with-nvidia-nemotron-speech-skills)). Combined with GPU‑PB on medical data, WER fell from 15.09 to 13.55, against 14.47 and 14.00 for each method alone ([TurboBias](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.07014v2)). One GitHub issue reports severe per-utterance deletions on Parakeet at `ngram_lm_alpha` ≥ 0.1 ([NeMo #14907](https://github.com/NVIDIA-NeMo/Speech/issues/14907)).

The archive's ~6,000 legacy transcripts are a large corpus, but they contain the very misspellings to be fixed. Apply the glossary's `misheard>right` map first, or drop sentences with known misheard forms, then build the LM on CPU and keep alpha below 0.1. This needs no cloud hardware.

### Fine-tuning: big gains, but it belongs after the bake-off

Fine-tuning gives the largest gains when the vocabulary is truly new. In NVIDIA's medical case study, ~150 h of synthetic TTS speech mixed with real speech **halved Nemotron's WER (24.03 → 12.06)** in about 14 GPU-hours on one GB300. Some prompts used near-miss distractors with the correct term missing a quarter of the time. Drug names "never heard in training" still failed. The recommended recipe for ~10 h of data is LoRA (dim 32) plus the encoder and projection, which needs about **65 GB + 0.95 GB per 1K batch tokens** of VRAM. That rules out anything smaller than an 80–96 GB card, so on Shape A it is roughly $17–$50 (estimate) ([Nemotron 3.5 report](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/nemotron/files/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3.5-Transcribe-Technical-Report.pdf)).

Whisper LoRA on about 22 h of synthetic entity audio cost about 7 A10G-hours ($13) per language and raised entity hit rate from 0.027 to 0.473. It also regressed read-prose WER by up to 9.4 points ([TTS–STT Flywheel](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.03073)). Parakeet's staged silver-then-gold recipe with English replay kept general WER within 0.14 points, while gold-only tuning more than doubled it ([NVIDIA HF blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/nvidia/fine-tune-asr-with-nvidia-nemotron-speech-skills)). Two conclusions follow. Fine-tuning must cover the whole 687-term glossary. And the 4 h gold set must stay a test set, not training data.

### Multi-system combination: vote with diverse systems, constrain any LLM

ROVER across independently trained systems is listed by NVIDIA as a "reliable" gain ([Nemotron 3.5 report](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/nemotron/files/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3.5-Transcribe-Technical-Report.pdf)). Voting also outvotes one system's prompt echo or "Khan". LLM fusion of N-best hypotheses barely helps zero-shot. Fine-tuned on synthetic rare-word error pairs with a simplified phonetic hint, it raised medical rare-word recall from 27.6% to 85.0%, though full IPA hints made results worse ([Yamashita et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.17410)). Unconstrained correction also "can generate entity phrases that are not even spoken" ([LOGIC](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.15397v1)), which supports the roadmap's constrained-edit stance.

### What the evaluation must measure

Biasing has to be judged by **term precision as well as recall**, alongside biased and unbiased WER. Score each condition with an oracle list and with the realistic per-talk list, and add bootstrap intervals. Fine-tuned Nemotron showed 1–6 points of run-to-run variance on small test sets ([Nemotron 3.5 report](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/nemotron/files/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3.5-Transcribe-Technical-Report.pdf)).

## Thirty runs plug into data/outputs without changing the contract

Every run below writes `data/outputs/{system}/{clip}.json` with the existing fields, so `eval/report.py` scores it unchanged. Record the exact list or prompt in `context` so the echo metric keeps working. `words` carries Parakeet's native timestamps, Nemotron's CTC word times through vLLM (unavailable with DFlash2 speculative decoding), or Granite's `[T:N]` tags, and is `null` elsewhere.

The context methods draw on the existing `transcriber/context/{id}.json`:

- **T**: the top 30 entries by `score` from `terms`, comma-separated and highest first.
- **H**: `hard_terms()`, i.e. difficulty ≥ 0.15.
- **G**: the whole glossary, a deliberate distractor stress test.
- **O**: the oracle list of glossary terms that occur in the corrected reference. This is available only once references exist and gives the upper bound.
- **F**: T pruned per 30 s VAD window by the Qwen3‑Omni presence judge.

Clips have `talk_id`, `start` and `end` in `eval/goldset/manifest.json`, which enables the **`@full` mode** that the 6-minute excerpts otherwise cannot test. The runner transcribes the whole source talk, keeps the segments or words that fall inside [start, end], and saves them under the clip's ID. Any 1-hour drift, passage skipping or loop then counts against the excerpt's score. Scoring boundary words with word timestamps, or allowing a few seconds of slack, avoids penalising segment edges.

The code work is modest. Add a generic **`asr/run_vllm.py`** that sends each clip (or VAD chunk) to a local vLLM server and writes the common JSON. Whether each model's transcription endpoint honours the `prompt` field is unverified, so the runner should fall back to chat-completions with an audio part and the model's documented template when it does not. Extend `run_parakeet.py` with `--beam`, `--c-unk` and `--ngram`, and add `--full-talk` to all runners. Add four metrics to `eval/report.py`: biased and unbiased WER, term precision/recall/F1 per context condition, a stock-phrase count on non-speech clips (Parakeet's hallucinations are 83% concentrated in its top 10 phrases ([HALAS](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.23048))), and a per-clip word-rate-vs-VAD-speech ratio that flags skipped passages without a reference.

Order matters more than breadth. Correct 15–20 references before tuning boosting weights or list lengths, because without them the matrix can only rank systems on speed, echo and non-speech output. The cloud outputs persist, though, so running the matrix first and re-scoring later costs nothing extra.

## Conclusion

The question has shifted from "which bigger model" to "which model ignores terms that were not said". Nemotron‑3.5 is the only open system trained for that, and parakeet‑ft‑v1 shows that how audio is chunked can be worth as much as a model swap. Both claims rest on vendor or single-developer evidence. A one-day, ~$30 rental is enough to confirm or refute them on this archive's audio, and the `@full` mode turns the existing 6-minute gold set into a long-form test at no labelling cost. If Nemotron's precision holds with the whole-glossary list (row 5), the per-talk term ranking in `build_context.py` becomes far less critical, because generous lists stop being dangerous.

The full run is a small expense next to the human labour of correcting references: under $350 for three diverse systems plus a vote. That changes the economics of the roadmap's later stages. Running a second and third system for ROVER costs less than an afternoon of manual correction, and a Nemotron LoRA on synthetic glossary audio costs tens of dollars. A sensible sequence is to correct the references, run the bake-off, take the vote, and fine-tune only if the remaining term errors cluster on glossary items that the acoustic models never hear correctly.
